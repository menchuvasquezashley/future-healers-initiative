What Do Surgeons Do?

Surgeons are physicians who diagnose and treat injuries, diseases, and other conditions. They may perform operations to treat problems such as injuries, tumors, and other conditions.

🏥 Where Do They Work?

Surgeons commonly work in hospitals, physicians' offices, and other clinical settings. Their work involves collaborating with nurses, physician assistants, other physicians, and many other healthcare professionals.

🎓 How Do You Become a Surgeon?

The path to becoming a surgeon takes many years of education and training.

College → Medical School → Surgical Residency → Practice

Physicians typically complete a bachelor's degree, followed by medical school. After medical school, physicians complete residency training in their chosen specialty. The length of residency depends on the specialty, and some surgeons complete additional fellowship training.

📅 What Can the Career Look Like?

A surgeon's work involves much more than performing operations. Surgeons may examine patients, review test results, develop treatment plans, communicate with patients and families, work with healthcare teams, and provide follow-up care.

Schedules can vary by specialty and workplace. Some surgeons work long or irregular hours and may be on call.
